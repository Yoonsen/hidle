Avenue de Tervueren, 188A, 4th Floor, Postbox 4, 1150 Brussels, Belgium Tel: +32 2 761 16 74 - Fax: +32 2 761 16 99 E-mail: essa@kellencompany.com Brussels, 22 December 2022 Subject: ESSA statement on trade with Norwegian Aquaculture products ESSA was informed recently that the government of Norway proposed to introduce a resource rent tax on aquaculture products. The deadline to submit input on the proposal is 3 January 2023. However, the new tax will already be introduced on 1 January 2023. We understand that the government of Norway proposes a resource rent of 40 percent including corporation tax, hence the total effective marginal tax will be approximately 62 percent. The introduction of the tax from 1 January 2023 is a major obstacle for the Norwegian salmon and trout industry, In practical terms, it will mean that the industry will face uncertainty with respect to f iscal regulations. It is our understanding that the final decision about the tax will be decided in the parliament of Norway in Q2 2023. This timing has major implications as the regulative framework for the industry will be uncertain for almost six months. ESSA was equally informed that a s a result of the proposal, there are almost no long-term contracts agreed upon between Norwegian aquaculture producers and domestic and international buyers. The main reason is that the tax rate is tied to the Nasdaq spot price, a price that only covers a minor share of Norwegian sales of salmon of superior quality. This price level is not representative because many producers sell their fish on longer-term fixed -price contracts. It is also our understanding that the prices in long -term contracts are usually a lot lower than the spot price, which may be very volatile. Since the tax is calculated according to the spot price, companies are reluctant to agree on long term contracts resulting in a large gap between the agreed fixed prices and the Nasdaq -price. As a result of the current situation, salmon producers are currently unwilling to agree to new contracts and sell their products only at spot prices to limit their tax risk. Hence, the European seafood industry buying and processing Norwegian aquaculture faces a challenging situation. The buyers have no predictability for prices and volumes of the raw materials for their business activities, which in next turn causes problems for the sales to consumers. ESSA would like to express major concern about the situation since the proposal of a new tax on Norwegian aquaculture production was introduced. We request and encourage urgent action in order to return to normal business activities and to secure a predictable situation for the buyers of Norwegian aqu aculture products. Laurent Lasquibar ESSA President
